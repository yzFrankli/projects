For question 10, please read pages 15--16 in *Programming Languages: Build,
Prove, and Compare* (the book section on two varieties of syntax). Then review
the WhileIterate rule on page 35.

10. At the end of your reading on page 16, it is suggested that all the while
    loops shown on page 15 could be represented with the same abstract syntax
    WHILE(e, s). Why, then, does the WhileIterate rule on page 35 use abstract
    syntax WHILE(e_1, e_2) instead of reusing WHILE(e, s)?

    (a) The difference is arbitrary; all that matters is the WHILE tag.

    (b) In Impcore, the condition and body of a while loop are both expressions,
        unlike the while loop examples shown on page 15.

    (c) The metavariable s and the metavariable e (with or without subscripts)
        represent the same kind of thing, so these two pieces of abstract syntax
        are equivalent.
    (d) Impcore's abstract syntax requires that all metavariables have 
        subscripts. 

    > (b)

$\rho'$ (ρ'), and a final state with ρ'{x ↦ v} ($\rho'\{x \mapsto v\}$).
